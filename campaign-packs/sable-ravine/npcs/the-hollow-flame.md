Once, long before Stonewatch existed, this shrine had a keeper. What's
left isn't really that person anymore — it's whatever's left over after
a very long time alone with something the shrine used to hold, slowly
losing the parts of itself that were ever human. It does not have a
body worth fighting; it has an old, cracked altar it can't leave, and a
long reach into anyone nearby who's afraid, superstitious, or hungry for
confidence they haven't earned.

**Run this as a conversation encounter, not a combat encounter.** The
Hollow Flame doesn't have hit points to reduce in any way that matters
narratively — the party's actual physical threat here is the guardian
(`encounters/shrine-guardian.md`), an animated undead thing bound to the
altar, statted as a real SRD creature and fought normally. The Hollow
Flame itself should be narrated, questioned, maybe bargained with or
defied, and the DM should feel free to improvise its half-finished
sentences and old grudges rather than script exact dialogue for it —
it's a mood and a threat, not a stat block to resolve_check against.

What actually stops it (DM's call, no single correct answer baked in
here on purpose): breaking whatever's left of its attention on the
world — destroying the altar, defeating the guardian and refusing
whatever bargain it offers, or simply leaving and cutting off the
"offerings" the goblins and kobolds have been unknowingly feeding it.
Any of these should meaningfully reduce its influence on the ravine
afterward; none of them need to be a single dramatic finishing blow.
