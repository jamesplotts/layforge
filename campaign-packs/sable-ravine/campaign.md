# The Sable Ravine

Three days' ride past the last farmsteads, the frontier keep of
**Stonewatch** holds a lonely stretch of border road with a garrison too
small for the job. A day's walk north, the land drops into the **Sable
Ravine** — a mile-long scar of black shale and switchback trails, riddled
with caves that something always seems to be living in, no matter how
many times the garrison clears one out.

For a generation the ravine's occupants kept to themselves: a goblin
raiding band skimming the road, a kobold warren that mostly just wanted
to be left alone, the odd wandering monster passing through. Manageable.
Survivable. Then, three months ago, the raids sharpened — coordinated,
confident, hitting supply wagons on schedule instead of by luck — and two
of the keep's scouts stopped reporting back.

Captain Orlen Vashti doesn't have the soldiers to spare for a real
punitive expedition, and doesn't especially want to explain to the
regional lord why the border road is quietly becoming impassable. She
wants outsiders — a small party, unaffiliated, deniable if it goes badly
— to go north, find out why the ravine's tenants have suddenly started
cooperating, find the missing scouts if they can, and end whatever's
organizing it.

## Hooks

- **The Captain's Offer** — Vashti hires the party outright: coin,
  supplies, and a standing invitation to garrison quarters, in exchange
  for answers and (ideally) a quiet end to the raids. The straightforward
  hook; assumes the party has some reason to be passing through
  Stonewatch already.
- **Kin in the Warren** — one party member has a personal reason to want
  into the Sable Ravine specifically — a missing relative among the
  scouts, an old debt to someone who vanished heading north, a rumor
  about what's actually in the ruined shrine at the ravine's heart. Use
  instead of or alongside the Captain's Offer for a party that wants a
  personal stake, not just a job.

## Running this pack

`shared_knowledge: strict` — the goblins, kobolds, and the shrine's
occupant do not know about each other's dealings with the party unless
the party (or the DM, deliberately) tells them. A party that cuts a deal
with the kobolds and *also* tells the goblins about it should watch that
information actually travel, not assume omniscience.

The throughline is that **something in the ruined shrine is the real
problem** (see `locations/ruined-shrine.md` and
`npcs/the-hollow-flame.md`) — the goblins and kobolds are symptoms, not
the cause, and a party that only ever fights its way through the ravine
without investigating the shrine will end the raids for a season at most.
